## Step 1: solve times per position (measurement; no engine change, no test stage)

`tools/examples/solve_times.rs` (new example; not counted in the tools
budget). From each of the 200 games in
`docs/tuning/speed_135_match.journal` it takes the first position with
exactly N empties and a legal move, replaying moves and passes and
tracking the colour to move. Each is solved through `search_tt` with
`endgame_threshold` N and limits of depth 64 and a 30,000 ms budget,
timed from the call to its return (so the win/draw/loss pass, the
full-score pass and the root's fallback evaluation are all inside).
Two conditions per position:

- cold: table cleared, then the solve;
- in game: table cleared, then the same side's previous move (the last
  earlier position with that colour to move and a legal move) searched
  with a 3,000 ms budget as a match worker would, then the solve on the
  same table without clearing.

It asserts that when both solves finish their scores agree, and prints
every position (board, both times, both solved flags, score) and per
condition the mean, 95th and 99th percentiles (nearest rank) and
maximum. Logs: `docs/tuning/solve_times_142_e{16,18,20}.log`, engine
sources of `0fa8d8a`, tools release profile, binary hash in each, load
about 1.4 throughout.

Deviation from the issue text: "in game" is the previous own move,
not every own move from 24 empties down. Reason, from the code: exact
search takes no cutoff from heuristic entries (`endgame_cutoff` reads
only `Domain::Endgame`) and no move hint (`hint` is `None` when
`exact`), so what earlier heuristic searches leave in the table can
only displace entries, and the previous own move is the last of them.
The numbers bear that out (below); a longer in-game history would cost
about 10 minutes per earlier move per empties count.

| Empties | Condition | Mean ms | 95th | 99th | Max | Over 3,000 ms | Over 12,000 ms | Not solved in 30 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | cold | 54.8 | 171.7 | 347.7 | 412.5 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 16 | in game | 56.1 | 173.1 | 348.8 | 413.2 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 18 | cold | 379.3 | 1,301.4 | 1,848.6 | 3,297.1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 18 | in game | 381.3 | 1,285.3 | 1,860.5 | 3,296.9 | 1 | 0 | 0 |

| 20 | in game | 3,706.0 | 10,511.3 | 24,053.6 | 30,000.0 | 73 | 7 | 2 |

The two unsolved 20-empties positions reached the 30 s cap in both
conditions and are counted at 30,000 ms in the mean, which therefore
understates the true mean.

## Step 3 applied to these numbers

The rule, amended in SKIS #142 at the user's decision before the
20-empties results were summarised: the largest N in {16, 18, 20}
whose in-game mean solve time is at most the move time (3,000 ms) and
whose in-game maximum on the 200 games is under the hard limit
(12,000 ms).

- 16: mean 56 ms, max 413 ms. Passes.
- 18: mean 381 ms, max 3,297 ms. Passes. One of 200 positions takes
  longer than the 3,000 ms move time, which the amended rule allows.
- 20: mean 3,706 ms (more than 3,000), max over 30,000 ms (more than
  12,000). Fails both conditions.

**Candidate: N = 18.** Stated now, before step 2 and before any game.
Under the current engine one game in 200 would play an arbitrary move
at 18; step 2's time limit and fallback are what make 18 usable.

## Step 2: what comes next

RED for the solve time limit and fallback follows review of this step.
Design I intend to propose there, for comment now: in `search_inner`'s
exact branch, first run a heuristic iterative search to a small fixed
depth (to be measured; aiming at a few milliseconds at 18 empties)
whose move becomes the fallback; then run the solve with its deadline
moved to four times the move time from the start of the move. If the
win/draw/loss pass finishes, behaviour is as now. If not, the
heuristic move is returned, unsolved. Where the move time comes from
and how `SearchLimits` carries the hard limit is to be settled in RED;
the node limit is unaffected.
