## Intended change (step 1 GREEN)

Replace the body of `pattern::features` with row tables:
`ROWS[row][byte]` is a `[u16; 46]` holding what the mover's discs in one
board row add to each instance's base-3 index; the opponent's add twice.
8 × 256 × 46 × 2 bytes = 188 KB, built once in a `LazyLock` from
`instances()`. `features` becomes 16 lookups and 16 array additions,
then adds each instance's offset. Signature, results, asset and trainer
unchanged. No `unsafe`, no intrinsics. The largest index is 3^10 − 1 =
59,048, so `u16` sums cannot overflow.

Then: read the assembly of the row-sum loop; if it is scalar, try small
source changes and keep one only if it is vectorised and measurably
faster. Measure `search_rate` again and report lines added. If the gain
is under 5%, revert.

Expected, from the scratch experiment recorded in the issue: `features`
about five times faster, search roughly 1.5 times.

## Review response: step 1 tests and measurement (round 1)

### Major: the speed gate

Accepted. `search_rate` divides the nodes of completed iterations by the
whole three seconds and mixes timed midgames with exact solves, so it
cannot decide a 5% question. The figure 2,709,457 above is that old
statistic and is not the speed baseline; its log is kept as it was.

New driver `tools/examples/search_bench.rs` (78 lines): a fixed, fully
completed workload on the corpus, cold table for every search, five
rounds, one thread.

- Midgame: the 12 corpus positions above the solve threshold, each to
  fixed depth 10. About 11 s per round.
- Exact: the 8 solve positions, 100 times each. About 2.3 s per round.
- It prints nodes and elapsed time per round and per workload the
  minimum, median, maximum and spread ((max − min) / min), and stops if
  the node total differs between rounds.

Rule, fixed before the comparison and written into the issue:

- the speed figure for a step is before-minimum / after-minimum on the
  midgame workload, with identical node totals;
- a run counts only if its five rounds are within 3% of each other;
  otherwise it is repeated on a quieter machine;
- a step is kept only if that figure is at least 1.05 and the slowest
  after-round is faster than the fastest before-round;
- the exact workload is reported separately and must not get more than
  3% slower;
- the three-second searches stay as a separate observation of depth
  reached (`search_rate`, depth only).

## Step 1 GREEN: row-table pattern indices

Gate approved in `docs/reviews/133.md` (re-review of `3440d28`).

### What changed

`src/pattern.rs` only. `features` now sums rows of a table:
`ROWS[row * 256 + byte]` is a `[u16; 46]` with what the mover's discs
`byte` on `row` add to each instance's base-3 index; the opponent's row
is added twice; offsets are added after widening to `u32`. The table is
a `LazyLock<Vec<[u16; 46]>>` built from `instances()`: 188,416 bytes,
about 0.2 ms on first use (measured, including building the instance
list). Signature, results, asset and trainer are unchanged. No `unsafe`,
no intrinsics. Engine production lines 1,017 → 1,025.

Reporting note from the review, adopted: the workloads clear the 64 MiB
table before every search, so the exact figure is cold-search workload
time including that reset, not the solver kernel alone. Table size and
reset are the same before and after.

### Vectorisation: a correction and the result

The issue and my earlier reports said the new loop compiled to scalar
code. That was wrong. I had read the per-crate assembly
(`cargo rustc --lib -- --emit asm`), and with `lto = "fat"` that is
emitted before the link-time pass in which the loop vectoriser runs.
The check that counts is the disassembly of the linked binary
(`objdump -d` on `tools/target/release/examples/search_bench`). There,
`pattern::features` uses NEON (`add.8h` on `q` registers); a standalone
copy of the loop compiled with `rustc -O` vectorises the same way. The
issue text is corrected.

Three source forms were compared in the linked binary, two benchmark
runs each, alternating:

| Form | `add.8h` instructions | Midgame min ms (two runs) |
|---|---|---|
| A: 46 lanes, `iter_mut().zip` (kept) | 12 | 7,335.6, 7,345.7 (and 7,177.5, 7,223.5 in separate runs) |
| B: rows padded to 48 lanes, same loop | 138 | 7,119.8, 7,216.5 |
| D: 48 lanes, `chunks_exact(8)` | 18 | 7,074.8, 7,216.2 |

All three are vectorised. B and D were 2 to 3% faster in the paired
runs, which is inside the 1 to 2% run-to-run variation seen for A
alone, and below the 5% bar. Neither is measurably faster, so the
simplest form is kept.

`legal_moves` and `flips` have no vector arithmetic in the linked
binary either; that part of the earlier finding stands, now checked the
right way. They are step 2.
