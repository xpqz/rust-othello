## Step 1 RED: null-window search

Intended behaviour: in `negamax` and `root`, the first move is searched
with the full window and each later move with `(alpha, alpha + 1)`; a
value strictly inside `(alpha, beta)` is searched again with the full
window. Midgame and exact search alike. Values returned at fixed depth
do not change; node counts fall.

Tests written:

- `tests/search.rs::window_equivalence` (new). On positions from random
  playouts (fixed seed, every seventh position with a legal move), with
  a pseudo-random evaluation (`noise`, a hash of the position, −2,000 to
  2,000) so that scores rarely tie and windows matter:
  - 1,000 positions with more than 8 empties: `negamax`, `search`,
    `search_limited` and `search_tt` (cold 65,536-entry table) each
    return the value of plain `minimax` (no ordering, no windows, no
    table) at the same depth;
  - 200 positions with 8 or fewer empties (`endgame_threshold: 8`):
    `search_limited` and `search_tt` return the exhaustive value;
  - in both groups the move `search_tt` returns is checked by searching
    its child with `minimax`: it must be worth the root value.

  Depths: 980 positions cycle through 1 to 4 and every fiftieth (20
  positions) is at depth 5. The issue asked for depths 1 to 5 evenly;
  that took 62 s in a debug build, this takes 15 s. Say if the even
  spread is wanted and I will make the test release-only instead.
  The test passes on the current engine by design: it pins what must

## Step 2 RED: table move from shallower searches

Intended behaviour: in midgame search, the move stored in a heuristic
table entry for the position is tried first whatever depth the entry
was searched to. Today `Entry::move_hint(depth)` returns it only when
the entry's depth is at least the current depth, so with iterative
deepening the previous iteration's move is ignored below the root.
Endgame entries still give no hint here (exact search is step 4). An
entry with no move gives none. Values at fixed depth do not change.

History: the restriction dates from #87 ("a deeper heuristic entry
supplies a move for ordering at a shallower depth, not the reverse").
I found no measurement behind it in the notes or reviews of #87 or #88.

  depth-8 nodes over the corpus at most 85% of `NULL_WINDOW_NODES`

- Unchanged and binding: `corpus_depths`, `golden_v1`,
  `window_equivalence`, `window_bounds`, `tt_equivalence`,

## Step 3 RED: evaluation ordering (conditional step)

Review of `5052631` approved step 2.

Design, picked on held-out positions as the issue requires
(`docs/tuning/order_heldout_135.log`; scratch copy with switches, node
counts and ordering-evaluation counts only):

- In midgame `negamax` at remaining depth 4 or more, children are tried
  in ascending order of: the child's evaluation (from the opponent's
  side, so lower is better for the mover) plus 300 centidiscs per
  opponent reply, with the table move counted 800 better. Ties by
  square. The corner and X/C ranks are not used there; the evaluation
  already knows them.
- The table move is a bonus, not an automatic first place. With it
  always first, evaluation ordering added nothing to step 2 (work 55.5 M
  against 54.2 M on sample A). As a bonus it gives 44.5 M (0.82 of
  step 2). The surface is flat: bonus 500 to 800, weight 200 to 450,
  threshold 4 to 6 all land between 44.5 M and 46.2 M. I took 800 / 300
  / 4 on sample A and ran them once on a disjoint sample B: 45.4 M
  against 56.6 M (0.80).
- Below depth 4, in exact search and at the root, ordering is as now.
  The root keeps its previous best first outright: `search_iterative`'s
  documented tie rule depends on it.
- "Work" counts an ordering evaluation as one node. That is a proxy;
  the gate is the benchmark. Expectation from it: about 1.2 on the
  midgame workload. If run against step 2 it does not reach 1.05 under
  the registered conditions, the step is reverted and reported as such.

Interface: `ordered_moves` gains a fifth argument,
`eval: Option<fn(Board) -> i32>`. `None` gives today's order exactly;
`Some` gives the order above. `negamax` passes `Some` only for midgame
nodes at depth 4 or more; `root` passes `None`.

Tests written:

- `tests/ordering.rs::evaluation_order` (new file). 200 playout
  positions with at least three moves; for every legal move as the
  preferred move, and for none, `ordered_moves(board, moves, 4,
  preferred, Some(noise))` must equal an independent sort by
  `(noise(child) + 300 × replies − 800 if preferred, square)`. It also
  requires that the preferred move comes first in more than 100 cases
  and not first in more than 100, so both sides of the bonus are
  exercised. It is in its own file because it cannot compile against
  today's four-argument function, and I did not want that to stop the
  other suites from running in RED.
- `tools/tests/golden.rs::fewer_table_nodes` (changed): a second
  condition, at most 90% of `HINT_NODES` = 1,406,429 (step 2's total on
  that workload; the reviewer reproduced the figure). Held-out depth-8
  nodes fell to 81%; this is a guard, not the gate.
- Unchanged and binding: `corpus_depths`, `golden_v1`,
  `window_equivalence`, `window_bounds`, the `tt_*` and `id_*` tests,
  and the five `ordering_*` tests in `tests/search.rs`, which describe
  the order without an evaluation.

## Step 3 GREEN: evaluation ordering

Review of `5341564` approved the step 3 tests, with one correction that
I accept: my RED note said the tableless `GOLDEN_V2` should not move.
That was wrong. Ordering by evaluation acts at any midgame node with
four or more plies left, table or no table, so tableless node counts
change too.

What changed:

- `src/search.rs::ordered_moves` takes a fifth argument,
  `eval: Option<fn(Board) -> i32>`. With `None` the key is as before

## Step 4 RED: exact search

Review of `bb3d4b0` approved step 3.

Intended behaviour, two changes to exact search:

1. Table move first. A position's table entry gives its stored move as
   the ordering hint whatever the entry's domain, in exact and midgame
   search alike. Today the hint is taken only in midgame search and
   only from heuristic entries. Both directions are sound for ordering:
   the move is only ever tried first among the generated legal moves,
   and no value crosses domains (`cutoff` and `endgame_cutoff` are
   untouched).
2. Under 5 empties, exact search orders moves without counting the
   opponent's replies (preferred move, corners, others, X/C squares,
   then square). From 5 empties up it is as now.
