## What Edax level 10 searches

Read from `LEVEL[10][empties]` in Edax's `src/search.c`: exact solve at
100% for 20 empties or fewer; depth 10 at selectivity 5 for 21 or more.
Selectivity 5 is `NO_SELECTIVITY` (`{999, 5, 100}` in
`selectivity_table`), so level 10's midgame search does not use ProbCut.
Level 12 by contrast is depth 12 at selectivity 0 (73%) above 24
empties. Edax's own output on the pilot positions reports depth `10`
with no `@` percentage, which agrees.

### 1 and 2. Nodes and speed at depth 10

```sh

  --example edax_gap -- --edax $EDAX > docs/tuning/edax_gap.log 2>&1
```

- nodes per second for each program (total nodes / total time) and the

- the count of games already won, drawn or lost at 24 empties (Edax's

- results that change after 24 empties measure the endgame schedules.

## Review response: setup and pre-registration (round 1)

All six findings accepted. Still nothing run beyond pilots.

### Major 1: depth and time together

`--depth N` (N < 64) with an explicit `--time` is now rejected in either
order: `--depth and --time are mutually exclusive`, exit 2, before any
journal is opened. `--depth` alone sets no deadline; the header is
`engine standard:d<N> time none`. Without `--depth` the header is the
old `engine standard time <ms>`, unchanged. `tools/tests/edax_depth.sh`
now also checks both argument orders are refused with no journal
written, and that a timed run against a fixed-depth journal stops with
`journal header mismatch`.

### Major 2: the analysis is now implemented

New `tools/examples/edax_phase.rs` (303 lines). For every game of a
ladder journal it asks Edax (`-solve`, level 60) for the exact value at
the first position with at most 24, 20 and 14 empties where the mover
has a move; the value at 14 is also solved by our own search and the run
stops if the two disagree. One record per game goes to its own journal,
so the pass resumes after a kill. It then requires both games of every
pair in `0..pairs` and nothing else, and prints:

- `final`: W/D/L, mean, pair-bootstrap interval (10,000 resamples, the
  existing `report_with`);
- `discs`: mean of the pair means m_i = (d_i0 + d_i1) / 2 and their
  sample standard deviation / sqrt(pairs);
- `exact24`, `exact20`, `exact14`: W/D/L of the exact outcome from the
  engine's side, mean with draws as half, pair-bootstrap interval;
- `change 24to20`, `20to14`, `14toend`: games whose outcome moved for
  and against the engine in each interval.

An interval is printed as `uninformative` when the existing policy says
so (fewer than 10 pairs or no spread between pairs); that is read as
"no difference shown".

Unit tests (5): `pair_error` uses a fixture whose game margins are +20,
−16, +30, −26 and whose pair means are both +2, and requires a pair
standard error of 0.00; `checkpoint_shares`, `constant_pairs`,
`incomplete_pair` (a missing game and a wrong pair count are both
errors) and `record_roundtrip`. `tools/tests/edax_phase.sh` plays four
pairs, runs the analysis, runs it again with SIGKILL after two records,
resumes, and requires the identical summary and eight records; it also
requires `--pairs 5` to be refused.

Cost: about 2.5 s per game here, so about 9 minutes for 200 games. That
is close to the ten-minute line, which is why the pass is resumable and
has the kill test.

The old `--attribute` mode of `edax_ladder.rs` is not used by this issue.

## Review response: round 2

### Major: a killed analysis left its Edax solve running

Accepted. `support/edax.rs::solve` now starts Edax under a small `sh`
supervisor: Edax runs in the background, a watcher reads a pipe whose
write end only the caller holds, and at end of file it sends Edax
SIGKILL. The pipe closes when the caller exits for any reason, SIGKILL
included, so no handler or `Drop` is involved. On a normal finish the
supervisor stops the watcher and returns Edax's status; the watcher
holds no output pipe, so reading Edax's output cannot block on it. Both
new examples use this one function.

RED first. With real Edax the orphan died within about 0.06 s in every
trial here, because Edax writes a progress line and takes SIGPIPE; that
depends on Edax writing and is not a guarantee, and it made a reliable
failing test impossible with the real binary. `tools/tests/edax_phase.sh`
therefore adds a stand-in solver that writes nothing: a script that
records its own PID and `exec`s `sleep 600`. The test kills the analysis
while that solver runs and requires the recorded PID to be gone within
0.5 s. Before the fix: `killed analysis left a silent solver running`.
After: passes. Only the recorded PID is inspected or signalled.

The real-Edax part of the test was tightened as asked:

- it uses a private copy of the binary so only its own solvers match,
  and its cleanup trap kills only those;
- it kills the analysis only once two records exist and a 24-empties
  solve is running, and requires that solve gone within 0.5 s;
- the wait loop has a timeout and stops if the analysis exits early;
- sorted journal records are compared as well as the summaries;
- at the end no process of the private copy may remain.

### Which part to target next

Speed, and #133 is written for it. The evidence:

- at equal depth Edax searches no fewer nodes than we do and processes
  ten times as many per second, so the per-node cost is the whole of its
  efficiency advantage at this level; ordering and pruning are not;
- the profile puts 43% of our time in one function, and a table-driven
  replacement tried in a scratch copy gave the same results at a fifth
  of the cost.

What speed will not buy: measurement 3 puts a doubling of time at under

the order of 90 Elo in self-play and probably less against Edax. And
measurement 4 shows a gap speed does not touch: at equal depth and no
solving we are at 0.4525 at 24 empties. That is the evaluator or move
choice, and it stays the larger open question after #133.

### Corrected conclusion (supersedes "Which part to target next")

What the measurements show, with their limits kept:

- On the 100 chosen positions Edax completed its nominal depth-10 search
  6.89 times faster than we did, while reporting more nodes than we
  count (ratio 0.68). Its reported-node rate is 10.09 times ours.
- The two programs' node counters are not a common unit. Edax's move
  ordering calls its own shallow evaluations and counts them; our
  mobility-based ordering does work without adding to `Ctx.nodes`. So
  the split into "nodes" and "speed" does not say that per-node cost is
  the whole of Edax's efficiency advantage, or that ordering and pruning
  contribute nothing. Only the time ratio is free of that convention,
  and it applies to these positions (30 to 40 empties, cold tables).
- The local profile is the evidence for what to try next: 43% of our
  search time is in `pattern::features`, and a table-driven version
  tried in a scratch copy returned identical results at about a fifth of
  the cost. That justifies #133's first step and nothing wider.
- Self-play gave 0.7050 at a time ratio of 46.9 and 0.6150 at 6.8:
  about 27 and 29 Elo per doubling over those ranges. Any figure for a
  tenfold speed-up at the 3,000 ms operating point would extrapolate a
  constant slope into a range of search effort that was not tested, and
  self-play says nothing certain about Edax. I withdraw the "about 90
  Elo" figure as a limit on what speed can buy; at most it illustrates
  the measured slope.
- The equal-depth match shows a deficit at 24 empties when depth is held
  at 10 for both (0.4525, interval below 0.5). It does not show that
  more depth cannot compensate in timed play, and I do not rank that
  deficit against a speed-up that has not been measured.

evidence paragraph of #133 has been reworded to match (reported-node
