## What Edax level 10 searches

Read from `LEVEL[10][empties]` in Edax's `src/search.c`: exact solve at
100% for 20 empties or fewer; depth 10 at selectivity 5 for 21 or more.
Selectivity 5 is `NO_SELECTIVITY` (`{999, 5, 100}` in
`selectivity_table`), so level 10's midgame search does not use ProbCut.
Level 12 by contrast is depth 12 at selectivity 0 (73%) above 24
empties. Edax's own output on the pilot positions reports depth `10`
with no `@` percentage, which agrees.

### 1 and 2. Nodes and speed at depth 10

```sh
cargo run --release --quiet --manifest-path tools/Cargo.toml \
  --example edax_gap -- --edax $EDAX > docs/tuning/edax_gap.log 2>&1
```

Defaults: 100 positions, depth 10, level 10, 3,000 ms. About 8 minutes,
sequential, nothing else running. Reported:

- node ratio (ours / Edax), median over positions and ratio of totals;
- nodes per second for each program (total nodes / total time) and the
  speed ratio;
- time ratio at depth 10 (ours / Edax), which is exactly node ratio
  times speed ratio on the totals;
- our depth reached in 3,000 ms on the same positions, and
  3,000 ms / our depth-10 time: the part of the 3,000 ms that buys depth
  beyond 10.

Reading: the ladder's time factor is 3,000 / 64.2 = 46.7. It is written
as (nodes) × (speed) × (time spent beyond depth 10). The three measured
parts are multiplied and compared with 46.7; the difference is reported
as unexplained. Known reasons for a difference: the positions cover 30
to 40 empties only, while Edax's 64.2 ms averages all its moves
including the exact solve from 20 empties; and both programs start cold
here but keep their tables within a game.

Limits stated in advance: Edax and our engine count nodes differently,
so the split between "nodes" and "speed" depends on convention; their
product, the time ratio, does not. Edax's time is reported to 1 ms.

### 3. Time scaling of our own engine

```sh
cargo run --release --quiet --manifest-path tools/Cargo.toml -- match \
  --a ab:10:3000 --b ab:10:64 --pairs 100 --seed 131 \
  --openings bench/openings_tune.txt --jobs 4 --max-load 6 \
  --out docs/tuning/time_scale_64.journal >> docs/tuning/time_scale.log 2>&1
```

and the same with `--b ab:10:440` and `time_scale_440.journal` (440 ms
is the geometric mean of 64 and 3,000). First 100 tune openings, paired
colours, pair bootstrap with 10,000 resamples. About 50 minutes each.
Void pairs are replayed by re-running the same command until 100 pairs
are valid. Reported: score, interval and the tool's Elo interval for
each. This is the value of a factor of 47 (and of 6.8) in time to our
engine against itself; self-play differences are usually larger than
differences against another program, and that is said with the result.

### 4. Equal-depth match

```sh
cargo run --release --quiet --manifest-path tools/Cargo.toml \
  --example edax_ladder -- --level 10 --depth 10 --pairs 100 --jobs 6 \
  --max-load 1000 --edax $EDAX \
  --out docs/tuning/edax_level10_d10.journal >> docs/tuning/edax_d10.log 2>&1
```

then the same with `--attribute --levels 10` added, to
`docs/tuning/edax_d10_attribution.log`. First 100 tune openings, book
off, one Edax thread. The load limit is disabled and six jobs are used
because the games are deterministic (`edax_depth.sh`); Edax's times from
this run are not used.

Both sides search depth 10 without selectivity while more than 20
squares are empty. They differ below that: Edax solves exactly from 20
empties, our engine from 14 and searches depth 10 from 15 to 20. So:

- the count of games already won, drawn or lost at 24 empties (Edax's
  exact value there) compares the two programs with equal nominal depth
  and no exact solving on either side: this is the evaluator comparison;
- results that change after 24 empties measure the endgame schedules.

Reported: score with interval, disc mean with its per-pair standard
error, the 24-empties table and the flips in each direction.

Reading fixed in advance, on the share of games already won at 24
empties (draws half), with its pair-bootstrap interval:

- interval entirely below 0.5: at equal depth our midgame play is weaker
  than Edax's, which at equal depth and no selectivity is evidence
  against our evaluator or move choice, not against search speed;
- interval containing 0.5: no difference shown at this sample size;
- interval entirely above 0.5: our midgame play is stronger at equal
  depth, and the ladder tie is explained by speed alone.

The pilot suggests our engine reaches beyond depth 10 in 3,000 ms, so I
expect the first outcome. That is a prediction, not a result.

## Review response: setup and pre-registration (round 1)

All six findings accepted. Still nothing run beyond pilots.

### Major 1: depth and time together

`--depth N` (N < 64) with an explicit `--time` is now rejected in either
order: `--depth and --time are mutually exclusive`, exit 2, before any
journal is opened. `--depth` alone sets no deadline; the header is
`engine standard:d<N> time none`. Without `--depth` the header is the
old `engine standard time <ms>`, unchanged. `tools/tests/edax_depth.sh`
now also checks both argument orders are refused with no journal
written, and that a timed run against a fixed-depth journal stops with
`journal header mismatch`.

### Major 2: the analysis is now implemented

New `tools/examples/edax_phase.rs` (303 lines). For every game of a
ladder journal it asks Edax (`-solve`, level 60) for the exact value at
the first position with at most 24, 20 and 14 empties where the mover
has a move; the value at 14 is also solved by our own search and the run
stops if the two disagree. One record per game goes to its own journal,
so the pass resumes after a kill. It then requires both games of every
pair in `0..pairs` and nothing else, and prints:

- `final`: W/D/L, mean, pair-bootstrap interval (10,000 resamples, the
  existing `report_with`);
- `discs`: mean of the pair means m_i = (d_i0 + d_i1) / 2 and their
  sample standard deviation / sqrt(pairs);
- `exact24`, `exact20`, `exact14`: W/D/L of the exact outcome from the
  engine's side, mean with draws as half, pair-bootstrap interval;
- `change 24to20`, `20to14`, `14toend`: games whose outcome moved for
  and against the engine in each interval.

An interval is printed as `uninformative` when the existing policy says
so (fewer than 10 pairs or no spread between pairs); that is read as
"no difference shown".

Unit tests (5): `pair_error` uses a fixture whose game margins are +20,
−16, +30, −26 and whose pair means are both +2, and requires a pair
standard error of 0.00; `checkpoint_shares`, `constant_pairs`,
`incomplete_pair` (a missing game and a wrong pair count are both
errors) and `record_roundtrip`. `tools/tests/edax_phase.sh` plays four
pairs, runs the analysis, runs it again with SIGKILL after two records,
resumes, and requires the identical summary and eight records; it also
requires `--pairs 5` to be refused.

Cost: about 2.5 s per game here, so about 9 minutes for 200 games. That
is close to the ten-minute line, which is why the pass is resumable and
has the kill test.

The old `--attribute` mode of `edax_ladder.rs` is not used by this issue.

### Major 3 and the revised reading (supersedes "4. Equal-depth match" reading)

Commands, with seed and invocation fixed:

```sh

  --example edax_ladder -- --level 10 --depth 10 --pairs 100 --jobs 6 \

  --out docs/tuning/edax_d10_phase.journal >> docs/tuning/edax_d10_phase.log 2>&1
```

Elapsed times from the six-job match are not used for anything.

What each number is taken to show, and no more:

- `exact24`: the theoretical state of the game at 24 empties after both
  programs chose every move by a depth-10 search without selectivity and
  without exact solving. Interval entirely below 0.5: under these
  conditions our play to that point is weaker than Edax's. Containing
  0.5, or `uninformative`: no difference shown at this sample size.
  Entirely above 0.5: our play to that point is stronger under these
  conditions.
- None of the three outcomes is read as explaining the ladder tie. The
  ladder had different depth histories (ours timed, usually deeper than
  10), warm tables and different endgame schedules, and this match
  changes all of those at once. An above-0.5 result would say only that
  the ladder tie is not evidence of a weaker evaluator.
- `change 24to20`: both sides still choose by depth-10 search, so this
  is more of the same comparison, not an endgame effect.
- `change 20to14`: Edax plays exactly, we search depth 10. Changes
  against us here are positions we held and gave away while Edax could
  not err; changes for us cannot occur if Edax's solve is exact, and any
  that appear are reported as a defect to investigate.
- `change 14toend`: both sides play exactly, so there should be none;
  any are reported as a defect.
- These are observed counts in games as played. They do not estimate
  what moving our solve threshold would gain.

The prediction stands: `exact24` below 0.5.

The time-factor table is an accounting identity on the 100 matched
positions (nodes × speed = time ratio at depth 10, exactly, on totals).
It is reported next to 46.7 with the difference named as unexplained,
and that limitation is kept whatever the equal-depth match shows.

## Review response: round 2

### Major: a killed analysis left its Edax solve running

Accepted. `support/edax.rs::solve` now starts Edax under a small `sh`
supervisor: Edax runs in the background, a watcher reads a pipe whose
write end only the caller holds, and at end of file it sends Edax
SIGKILL. The pipe closes when the caller exits for any reason, SIGKILL
included, so no handler or `Drop` is involved. On a normal finish the
supervisor stops the watcher and returns Edax's status; the watcher
holds no output pipe, so reading Edax's output cannot block on it. Both
new examples use this one function.

RED first. With real Edax the orphan died within about 0.06 s in every
trial here, because Edax writes a progress line and takes SIGPIPE; that
depends on Edax writing and is not a guarantee, and it made a reliable
failing test impossible with the real binary. `tools/tests/edax_phase.sh`
therefore adds a stand-in solver that writes nothing: a script that
records its own PID and `exec`s `sleep 600`. The test kills the analysis
while that solver runs and requires the recorded PID to be gone within
0.5 s. Before the fix: `killed analysis left a silent solver running`.
After: passes. Only the recorded PID is inspected or signalled.

The real-Edax part of the test was tightened as asked:

- it uses a private copy of the binary so only its own solvers match,
  and its cleanup trap kills only those;
- it kills the analysis only once two records exist and a 24-empties
  solve is running, and requires that solve gone within 0.5 s;
- the wait loop has a timeout and stops if the analysis exits early;
- sorted journal records are compared as well as the summaries;
- at the end no process of the private copy may remain.

### Minor: `--depth 64`

`--depth` now takes 1 to 60 and anything else is refused, so supplying
the option always means fixed-depth mode. `edax_depth.sh` checks that
`--depth 64` is refused and writes no journal.
