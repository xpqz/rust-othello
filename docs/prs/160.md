# Issue 160: Time management design

Design work for epic #151, with no test stage: `docs/design/time.md`, for
review before any code. No child issues have been created; they follow
approval, as the epic requires.

## Summary

- **Engine:**
  - `SearchLimits.start_ms`: no iteration starts after it. `new` and
    `depth` set it equal to `time_ms`, so flat play is unchanged.
  - `allot(board, remaining, threshold)`: stage-weighted allocation, with
    a solve reserve and a worst case of R/2 per move, apart from the
    existing unbounded fallback.
  - `Agent::clock` (a default no-op) and `clock_agent`.
  - About 25–35 lines.
- **Tools:**
  - a clock belongs to an agent (`pc:80:c<ms>`). The driver times both
    sides in-process, a clocked side that goes below zero flags and
    loses, and load voiding covers the clock control;
  - a `+book` suffix for #156;
  - journal fields `black_ms white_ms flag`.
  - **Tools budget:** raised (user decision). I propose a ceiling of 1,400.
- **Gate:**
  - the candidate's clock C equals today's flat player's measured mean
    thinking time per game, taken from a pre-registered 50-pair pilot
    (#142 evidence: 52.8 s);
  - the baseline is `pc:80:3000`, unclocked;
  - 400 pairs on a fresh `bench/openings_time.txt`, one look, lower bound
    above 0.500;
  - about 6 h.

## Evidence

Per-side thinking time from `docs/tuning/threshold_142_match.journal`
(400 side-games at 3,000 ms per move): mean 52.8 s, median 52.0 s, 90th
percentile 55.3 s, max 63.4 s. The longest move was 12.0 s, the 4×
exact-solve allowance.

## User input folded in

- **Tools budget:** "Raise tools budget".
- **Fairness** (user, Nov 18): "the 3-second-per-move baseline only uses
  about 53 of its 90 seconds, so the new player gets more thinking time."
  The draft's equal 90 s clocks were replaced. The candidate's whole clock
  is now the baseline's measured mean usage, and the baseline is
  unclocked.

## Review response (revision 2)

`docs/design/time.md` revision 2 answers each finding of
`docs/reviews/160.md`. The doc's "Review response" table maps every
finding to its change. In short:

- **Allocation:**
  - integer `u64` ms, rounded down;
  - `start_ms = max(1, min(t, time_ms))`, so the start cutoff never
    exceeds the deadline (e = 19, R = 1,000 gives 250/250);
  - a tiny-clock rule;
  - examples now give both the start cutoff and the in-search deadline;
  - the exact branch ignores `start_ms` and keeps its allowance;
  - a controlled slow-evaluator test for the start limit.
- **Clock contract:** return-and-forfeit.
  - Each `choose` call is charged in full, book hits and overhead
    included. Passes and opening moves cost nothing.
  - Time accumulates as a `Duration`. A flag is `used > C`, strictly; the
    flagging move is discarded and still charged.
  - A sub-millisecond remainder is passed to the agent as 0.
  - The harness gets an injected time source for deterministic tests.
  - The doc states that engine deadlines are settings, not bounds.
- **Gate:**
  - the pilot is bound to the build, openings and seed before it runs;
  - C is taken from complete accepted pairs and never recomputed;
  - pre-registered equal-time condition: the candidate's realized mean is
    at most 1.05 × the baseline's in the same pairs, flags included;
  - enabling needs both the score and equal-time. Otherwise the result is
    reported for the tested controls only, with no equal-time claim;
  - #142 is now described as a pooled approximation (51.5 s at threshold
    18).
- **Resumption:**
  - load is sampled once per pair, and the whole pair is void, flagged or
    not; ten void passes stop the run;
  - void totals come from a retained stderr log;
  - flag points are kept separate from the disc score;
  - resume keeps accepted pairs and replays the rest with the same seeds.
    Identical results are tested with injected time and load;
  - the `c<ms>` and `+book` grammar is defined, with its refusals;
  - depth-by-stage reporting is deferred.
