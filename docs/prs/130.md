## Driver

`tools/examples/edax_ladder.rs` is outside the tools production count.
It has three modes:

- `--bridge`: the 20-game check above.
- `--level N`: the 50-pair level run, journaled.
- `--ladder`: levels 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 in ascending order, stopping
  after the first level whose mean score is below 0.25.

The journal is one file per level, default
`docs/tuning/edax_level<N>.journal`. Records are appended per pair; a
pair is replayed if either game is missing, or if the measured load
average exceeds `--max-load` (void records are appended, then discarded
and replayed). Re-running the same command resumes from the journal.
`--jobs` defaults to 4 and `--max-load` to 6.

The pilot used the release example at 3,000 ms per engine move.

## Review response: ladder run 1 and fixes

The first ladder attempt from `cb1f5ce` stopped after level 1 with a
journal-header mismatch. It played 38 valid pairs (75 wins, 1 loss,
mean 0.9868) before the defect surfaced; those 38 pairs are **not** a
calibration result and are not carried forward. The failed log and
journal remain in the `othello-edax-ladder` worktree.

Fixes:

- `header()` no longer contains the date. The header now holds only
  level, engine, time, Edax path and hashes, commit, openings and hash,
  jobs and max load. The date is printed in the log (`level N date ...`)
  and by `run_ladder.sh` before the command.
- `tools/tests/edax_resume.sh` is the interrupt-and-resume test. It runs
  a one-pair level, sleeps one second, runs the same command again, and
  requires the same summary line and the same journal record count.
  RED at `cb1f5ce`: the second run fails with the header mismatch
  (`docs/tuning/edax_resume_red_cb1f5ce.log`). GREEN after the fix:
  `docs/tuning/edax_resume_green.log`, same summary and no replay.
- Edax move time is accumulated as a `Duration` in microseconds and
  converted once. The level-1 mean is now reported as 0.02 ms per move
  rather than 0.00.
- The void progress line now prints the pair id, not the completion
  count.
- Stop rule: if a level still has missing pairs after ten attempts,
  `run_level` returns an error. An incomplete level is never reported as
  a result.

The old journal's header format will not match the fixed header, so the
re-run starts level 1 afresh. The existing `docs/tuning/edax_level1.journal`
in the pinned worktree must be removed or moved aside before that run.

### What "level L" means here

From Edax's own `LEVEL` table (`src/search.c`), at 24 empties the level
searches to depth L for levels 1-10; level 12 switches to its endgame
solver at 24. The last empty count where depth reaches empties (its
endgame-solve start) is 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 and 24 empties respectively.
The corresponding selectivity is the no-selectivity value 5 (100%) for
levels 1-10; level 12 uses selectivity 3 (98%) at 24-22 empties.

| Level | depth at 24 empties | endgame solve from | selectivity at solve |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 2 empties | 100% |
| 2 | 2 | 4 | 100% |
| 4 | 4 | 8 | 100% |
| 6 | 6 | 12 | 100% |
| 8 | 8 | 16 | 100% |
| 10 | 10 | 20 | 100% |
| 12 | 24 | 24 | 98% |

### Time per move at levels 10 and 12

The journals show the level-12 mean was not caused by load:

| | level 10 | level 12 |
|---|---|---|
| Edax moves/game | 25.79 | 26.02 |
| Mean load | 4.66 | 4.65 |
| Mean total Edax ms/game | 1655.7 | 1121.7 |
| Median total Edax ms/game | 1488 | 831 |
| Correlation, total time vs load | 0.21 | 0.12 |
| Mean time/move | 64.2 ms | 43.1 ms |

The per-move times were not recorded, so the remaining difference cannot
be assigned from the journals alone. The only structural difference is
the level table above: level 12 starts its selective endgame solve at 24
empties, while level 10 searches depth 10 at 22-24 and solves from 20.
That is a candidate, not a proven cause; the load correlation and the
mean loads rule out machine load.

### Disc mean

Level 10's score is level while its disc mean is −3.85 because wins and
losses are asymmetric:

- level 10: mean +16.55 discs in wins, −24.41 in losses (49 each);
- level 12: mean +16.19 in wins, −23.44 in losses (43 wins, 55 losses).

The engine wins narrow games and loses wide ones; the game score hides
that board-control gap.

### Loss attribution

For every loss at levels 10 and 12 I replayed the journal moves and took
the first position with 24 or fewer empties, then asked Edax for its
exact value. Edax was run with:

`-solve <position.obf> -level 60 -verbose 1 -n 1 -book-usage off -eval-file data/eval.dat`

The OBF board was written from our board with black as `X`, white as `O`
and the side to move as the trailing `X`/`O`. A loss whose exact value
from the engine's side was negative at that position is counted as
already lost; otherwise it was lost later.

- 104 losses total;
- **96 already lost at 24 or fewer empties**;
- 8 lost later;
- level 10: 47 of 49 already lost, 2 later;
- level 12: 49 of 55 already lost, 6 later.

Five spot checks at positions with 14 or fewer empties reached later in
the same games agreed exactly with our own solver on score and side to
move, 5/5. The attribution therefore points at midgame/early-endgame
evaluation and move choice, not final conversion. Deeper endgame solving
alone would not recover these games; level 12's earlier selective
endgame solve did not prevent the same pattern.

### Reading against the engine programme

The standard engine at 3 s is competitive with a shallow depth-limited
Edax level 8 but loses to level 10's depth/evaluation and level 12's
selective endgame schedule. Since 96 of 104 losses are already decided
by 24 empties, the next strength work should target midgame evaluation
and search selectivity/data, not a deeper exact endgame. The pattern
evaluation gate (#126) is the relevant lever; simply raising the exact
endgame threshold would address at most the 8 later losses.
