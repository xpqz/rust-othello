## Step 3 RED: constant-time position key

Review of `36b59ba` approved keeping step 2 as a marginal gain. The
accumulated comparison it asks for (start-of-issue binary against
final binary, alternating) is owed before final review and will not be
replaced by multiplying step figures.

Intended change: `tt::zobrist(board)` becomes
`splitmix64(board.me ^ splitmix64(board.opp))`, using the `splitmix64`
already in `src/tt.rs`. Name and signature stay. The per-square key
tables (`ZOBRIST`, `tables`) and the per-disc loop (`mix`) go. The
form is not symmetric in the two sides, so a position and its
colour-swapped twin get different keys; `splitmix64` is a bijection on
64 bits, so for a fixed opponent bitboard distinct mover bitboards
never collide, and across opponents a collision is a 2^-64 event as
before. Key 0 stays the empty-slot marker, with the same negligible
chance of a real position hashing to it as today.

Tests:

- `tests/tt.rs::zobrist_keys` (changed): the pinned start-position key
  is now `0x33e1_ce12_151b_5cfc`, computed independently of the engine
  (the formula above evaluated in Python). The other assertions
  (non-zero, repeatable, differs from the colour-swapped start) are
  unchanged. **Fails today**, as intended: the engine returns the old
  key `0xe63a_89a2_65d0_0287`.
- `tests/tt.rs::key_spread` (new): at least 200,000 distinct
  positions, from random playouts together with each one's
  colour-swapped twin. Every key is non-zero and no two are equal. The
  low 10 bits and the high 10 bits each fall into 1,024 buckets, and
  every bucket must hold between 3/4 and 5/4 of the even share (about
  195; a binomial count there has a standard deviation of about 14, so
  the band is ±3.5 sd for 2,048 buckets). The table index is the key
  modulo the table length, so the low bits are what matter for a
  power-of-two table; the high bits are checked so a later change of
  indexing does not meet a surprise. Passes today with the old key, by
  design: it pins a property the old function has and the new one must
  keep.
- Unchanged and binding: `corpus_depths`, `golden_v1`, `golden_v2`
  (tableless, so its node counts must not move), `window_equivalence`,
  `window_bounds`, the `tt_*` tests, `solver_reuses_endgame_bounds`,
  `fewer_table_nodes` (must still pass; its total will be reported).
- Three tests use `zobrist` as a source of pseudo-random evaluations
  (`noise` in `tests/search.rs` and `tests/ordering.rs`). Their
  expected values are computed from the same function inside the test,
  so they stay self-consistent; `evaluation_order`'s requirement that
  the preferred move leads in more than 100 cases and trails in more
  than 100 depends on the noise and I will report its counts.

Expected failure now: `zobrist_keys` only; `cargo test --test tt`:
8 passed, 1 failed. Engine fmt and clippy clean.

Node counts on the benchmark will change (entries land in other
slots): pilot 8,614,615 → 8,496,615 midgame and 7,000,900 → 7,001,000

move.

## Review response: step 3 tests (review of `4cfb02e`)

Accepted. The two ten-bit histograms left index bits 10 to 21
unchecked, and the issue asked for the 22-bit index.

- `tests/tt.rs::key_spread` (changed). Keys of the 200,000-plus sampled
  boards are collected by `sample_keys`. New criterion `spread`, over
  the actual index `key % MAX_ENTRIES` (2^22 slots): at least 97% as
  many slots used as there are keys, and no slot with more than 6 keys.
  Both bounds come from the even-throw model and were fixed before
  looking at the new function: with n keys in m slots the expected
  number of used slots is about n − n²/2m, 97.6% of n here, with a
  standard deviation of a few tens of slots; and the chance that any
  slot holds seven or more is about m·λ⁷/7! with λ = n/m ≈ 0.048,
  under 10⁻¹². The full-key checks (non-zero, all distinct) and the two
  ten-bit histograms stay.
- `tests/tt.rs::clustered_keys_rejected` (new): the probe asked for.
  The same keys with bits 10 to 21 cleared land in 1,024 slots;
  `spread` must reject them. It does (and the ten-bit histograms alone
  would not have).
- Wording corrected: the bijection gives no collision between distinct
  mover bitboards for a fixed opponent. Across different opponents,
  "2^-64" is what an ideal uniform hash would give; it is a modelling
  assumption about `splitmix64`, not a guarantee.
- Not taken: a frozen noise function for the search fixtures. They
  compare against minimax under the same evaluator inside each test, so
  they stay valid; I will report any count that moves.

Results now: `cargo test --test tt`: 9 passed, `zobrist_keys` fails as

## Step 3 GREEN: constant-time position key

Review of `23f3f0c` approved the step 3 tests.

**One approved assertion was mis-specified and is replaced; please
review this first.** `key_spread`'s ten-bit histogram check required
every one of 2,048 buckets to hold between 3/4 and 5/4 of the even
share (195), which I had called "±3.5 sd". For an ideal hash about one
bucket in 2,048 falls outside ±3.5 sd by chance, so that check fails
about 60% of the time on a perfect key; the old key passed it by luck.
With the new key one low-bits bucket held 248 (limit 243, +3.8 sd) and
the test failed. The statistics for both functions on the same
200,064 boards:

| | Low 10 bits: min / max / chi² | High 10 bits: min / max / chi² | Slots used, most in one |
|---|---|---|---|
| Old key | 149 / 239 / 961 | 157 / 235 / 951 | 195,300, 3 |
| New key | 153 / 248 / 1,020 | 151 / 236 / 990 | 195,369, 4 |

Chi-squared over 1,024 buckets has mean 1,023 and standard deviation
about 45 for an even spread; all four values are within one deviation
of the mean or below it. I replaced the per-bucket band with
`chi² < 1250` (five deviations above the mean) for each histogram.
The limit comes from the distribution, but I did choose it after
seeing these numbers, and I say so. The 22-bit slot criterion (97% of
slots distinct, at most 6 per slot), the full-key checks, and the two
rejection probes are exactly as approved and pass unchanged.
