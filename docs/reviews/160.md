# Review of #160 — Time management design

## Design review of `ca317b51`

**Request changes.** A stage-weighted allocator with a separate iteration
start limit is a reasonable candidate for the one-look gate. The draft
needs a consistent allocation formula, an enforceable clock contract,
and a rule for interpreting strength when the pilot and gate consume
different amounts of time.

### Major — Make the allocation bounds consistent and testable

At `docs/design/time.md:80–96`, take T=18, e=19 and R=1,000 ms. Then
m=1, w=1.5 and t=300 ms, while the proposed hard limit is 250 ms.
The driver could admit a new iteration after its own move deadline.
At R=1–3 ms, the positive 1 ms minimum also conflicts with R/4.
Specify rounding, arithmetic range and the tiny-clock rule. Clamp the
iteration start cutoff to the effective search deadline. State whether
the examples refer to the start cutoff or the in-search deadline. At
the opening, for example, about 2.1 s is the start cutoff and about
4.2 s is the move deadline.

The proposed zero-start test and equal-limit test can pass with an
implementation that applies the soft deadline inside the search.
Add a controlled test that admits an iteration before the cutoff, lets
it finish after that cutoff but within the move deadline, and rejects
the next iteration. Make the exact branch's treatment of start_ms
explicit, including that the flat solver keeps its existing allowance.

### Major — Define the clock's actual enforcement and flag semantics

At `docs/design/time.md:99–108` and `:175`, the engine cannot promise
the candidate will never consume more than C. The exact fallback uses
NO_DEADLINE and an unlimited node budget. Ordinary deadline polling
also permits overrun. A forfeit after choose returns enforces the score,
while the run still consumes the expired move's elapsed time. The
quarter/half figures are deadline settings, not worst-case elapsed
times. A flag can also follow scheduling delays, initialization or the
minimum allocation near zero. The load filter is a screening rule and
does not rule these out.

Choose and document either a clock-only bounded fallback with a legal
last-resort move, or a return-and-forfeit contract that explicitly permits
elapsed overruns and counts them. Either can preserve flat behavior.
Define the exact-zero rule, whether a flagging move is discarded before
application, and which time is charged on passes and book hits. A timer
around choose necessarily charges lookup and call overhead. Charging it
is the simplest rule and gives consistent accounting for the later book
gate. Keep fractional elapsed time across moves, converting to integer
milliseconds only for allocation and reporting. Specify how positive
remaining time below 1 ms is handled. Use a fake clock or supplied
durations to test cumulative debits, boundary flags, the discarded
flagging move, and pass/book handling without relying on scheduler
timing.

### Major — Pre-register what the equal-time gate can conclude

At `docs/design/time.md:160–195`, setting C to a separate pilot's mean
equalizes a candidate cap with an estimate of the baseline's usage.
It does not equalize realized time. Baseline thinking time can change
with the new opponent and fresh openings. An early flag can also truncate
the baseline's game and lower its measured mean. Reporting the mismatch
after the fact, while enabling on score alone, leaves an equal-time
claim possible even if the candidate consumed materially more.

Keep the pilot and the fixed C rule, but pre-register how actual time
and flags qualify the strength decision. For example, define in advance
a maximum excess in candidate elapsed time over the accepted paired
baseline games, including expired moves, and state how flagged pairs
affect that interpretation. If the condition is missed, report the
strength result as a comparison at the tested controls and withhold an
equal-time allocation claim. Define the pilot aggregation over complete
accepted pairs, bind its openings and build before measuring C, and do
not recalculate C from gate data. Report both realized means and their
paired difference. The assumed threshold-18 baseline should be described
as an approximation from the old match until the current-build pilot
is complete.

### Major — Specify what survives clocked match resumption

At `docs/design/time.md:134–150` and `:227–230`, the current driver samples
load once after both games and accepts or discards the whole pair. State
the sample point, pair retry rule, retry exhaustion policy, and whether
that policy applies equally to flagged and unflagged games. The existing
journal discards failed attempts, so reporting void and replay totals
requires a retained log or an explicit count record across restarts.
Keep numeric flag scoring separate from the disc score and define the
representation of a game stopped before terminal. Test both colours and
saved-report aggregation.

Real-time games need not produce identical fresh results under SIGKILL
and resume. Require preserving complete accepted pairs, retrying partial
or void pairs with the same openings and seeds, rejecting changed clock
settings, and reproducing the report from the completed journal. An
identical-results test belongs in a deterministic fixture with injected
elapsed time and load. Define the suffix grammar for clock versus
`--nodes` and inherited depth fields and test the intended refusals.
The existing `Agent::choose` returns only a square, so specify how
depth-by-stage reaches the report and survives resume, or defer that
diagnostic explicitly.
