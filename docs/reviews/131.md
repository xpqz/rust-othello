## Findings

### Major: Mixed depth/time options can silently reuse an incompatible journal

Location: `tools/examples/edax_ladder.rs:88-92`, `:324-326`, `:526-532`.

`--depth N` sets `time = NO_DEADLINE`, but a subsequent `--time T` overwrites
that value. The game then uses both limits, while the journal header still
says `standard:dN time none` whenever `N != 64`. The reverse argument order
has different behavior. Thus two different effective searches have the same
header and can be combined on resume without a mismatch error.

Reproduced without playing any games: invoke the committed release driver
with `--level 1 --pairs 0 --jobs 1 --max-load 1000`, a temporary output journal,
and first `--depth 4 --time 0`, then `--depth 4` against the same journal. Both
exit successfully and use the identical header prefix:

```text
header v1 level 1 engine standard:d4 time none
```

The first effective search has a zero deadline; the second has no deadline.
This is a provenance and resume correctness problem even though the planned
command happens not to combine the flags.

Reject simultaneous explicit `--depth` and `--time` in either order, or define
order-independent semantics and record every effective limit accurately.
Add argument-order and incompatible-resume tests. Keep the ordinary timed
header compatible where its effective configuration is unchanged.

### Major: The pre-registered decision depends on analysis that is not implemented

Location: `docs/prs/131.md:136-147`;
`tools/examples/edax_ladder.rs:669-715`, `:855-1017`.

The decision rule uses a pair-bootstrap interval for the score at 24 empties,
and the report promises the final disc mean with its per-pair standard error.
The committed paths do not calculate either quantity:

- `run_level_once` bootstraps the final game score and prints the final disc
  mean, without a disc standard error.
- `attribute` emits individual exact-24 states and aggregate counts/flips. It
  neither groups those states into opening pairs nor computes their interval.

The raw records contain enough information, so no engine change is needed.
Add a committed, tested analysis command or script that joins by pair/game
ID, verifies two valid games per requested pair, and produces the actual
statistics used by the pre-registered rule. Record the exact invocation and
bootstrap seed in the notes before running the production jobs.

For final disc margins, define `m_i = (d_i0 + d_i1) / 2` and use the sample
standard deviation of `m_i` divided by `sqrt(number_of_pairs)`. Bootstrap the
mean of the two exact-24 W/D/L points per pair using the existing interval
policy. Test a fixture whose individual-game margins vary but whose pair
means are constant, so treating games as independent cannot pass unnoticed.
State how an uninformative interval is handled.

### Major: The pre-registration overattributes phase changes and the ladder tie

Location: `docs/prs/131.md:127-147`.

Two conclusions exceed what these controls establish:

1. A change after 24 empties does not by itself measure the difference in
   endgame schedules. From 24 down to 20 empties both programs still choose
   moves using their depth-10 heuristic searches. Their evaluators or move
   choices can change the theoretical outcome during that interval before
   either root solver starts. Changes later also need careful interpretation.
2. An exact-24 interval above 0.5 would show stronger play to that checkpoint
   under these equal nominal-depth conditions. It would not establish that
   the original ladder tie is explained by speed alone: the ladder has
   different depth histories, warm tables, and different endgame schedules.
   The already-noted sampling differences also remain.

Keep the useful comparison, but pre-register conclusions at the strength
supported by it. Either label the flips as combined post-24 play, or add
checkpoints at 20 and 14 empties to distinguish the interval with equal
heuristic schedules from the interval with different solve triggers. Even
with those checkpoints, stage attribution is observational rather than a
counterfactual estimate of the gain from changing a threshold.

The time-factor product is a useful accounting identity on the matched
positions. It is not independent confirmation that the original all-game
factor of 46.7 has one causal explanation. Preserve the stated residual and
sampling limitations in every outcome, including an above-0.5 result. The
prediction of a below-0.5 result can remain unchanged.

### Minor: Timed-search nodes and the extra-time factor need narrower labels

Location: `tools/examples/edax_gap.rs:185-213`, `:230-237`;
`src/search.rs:368-390`; `docs/prs/131.md:78-84`.

`SearchResult.nodes` here retains the cumulative count at the last completed
heuristic iteration. Nodes from the interrupted next iteration are discarded
when `root` returns `None`. Dividing `timed.nodes` by the whole elapsed time
therefore does not measure actual search throughput. The fixed-depth
`ours_nps` calculation is unaffected because that search completes.

Omit the extra timed NPS field or label it as completed-iteration nodes per
elapsed second. Also call `timed_us / fixed_us` a budget/time ratio, not time
that necessarily buys depth beyond 10: an interrupted iteration can consume
that time while leaving the completed depth at 10 or below. The logged depths
permit reporting how many positions finish below, at, or above depth 10.

### Minor: Make the standalone collector more self-describing and fail closed

Location: `tools/examples/edax_gap.rs:132-174`, `:193-203`;
`docs/prs/131.md:57-67`.

The new collector's output has position indices but no source game IDs or
boards, no input-journal hash, and no automatic commit/binary/evaluation-data
header. The notes propose adding commit/date first, but the displayed `>`
redirection would erase a pre-existing preamble.

Prefer emitting provenance directly, including actual board/game identifiers
and the full configuration. Check Edax's exit status and require the returned
depth/selectivity to match the intended non-selective depth-10 run. Require
the requested number of positions before measuring. The committed fixture
does currently provide 100 positions, as the unit test confirms; these checks
would protect the measurement from input/configuration mistakes.

### Minor: Level 12 is the first selective level in this ladder, not in Edax

Location: Edax `src/search.c`, `LEVEL[11]`.

`LEVEL[11][30] = {11, 0}` already enables selectivity above 24 empties.
Level 12 is the first such level among the tested ladder levels. This does
not change any planned level-10 measurement, but use the qualified wording
when correcting the earlier claim.

## Answers to the review questions

- **Six jobs with the load limit disabled for the fixed-depth match:**
  acceptable once the limit/header issue is fixed. Both sides use fixed work,
  Edax uses one thread and fixed-level mode, and the committed short
  repeatability test passes. Do not use elapsed times or NPS from this
  concurrent run. Keep the node/speed measurements sequential and quiet.
- **The 100 positions at 30–40 empties:** a reasonable, reproducible matched
  sample for a midgame depth-10 comparison. The selection rule is fixed and
  one position is taken per game. Keep the existing qualification that this
  sample does not represent Edax's whole-game 64.2 ms average. No sample
  change is requested because of the four-position pilot. If uncertainty
  intervals are later added, remember that two source games can belong to
  the same opening pair.
- **Long-job lifecycle:** the existing ladder resume/SIGKILL test passes
  with the changed driver. The planned collector is below the ten-minute
  threshold. Confirm the separate exact-attribution pass stays below it;
  it invokes new solves and is not merely a cheap report over saved output.
  If its estimate exceeds ten minutes, give that pass its own resumable
  units and interrupt/resume test before running it.
