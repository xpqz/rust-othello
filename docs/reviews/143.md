### Major: working-set timing does not establish the cache mechanism or a practical floor

Location: `docs/prs/143.md:46–57`.

The microbenchmark repeats a varied board array versus the first 16 boards
of that array. Both conditions repeatedly access the same finite `ROWS`
table, with five passes per condition; no cache flush or cache-miss/stall
measurement establishes a cold-table condition. Thus similar feature
throughput is useful evidence of little sensitivity to this particular
working-set change, but does not establish that extraction is not
memory-bound. Cache-resident loads can still be limited by load throughput,
latency or dependencies. Counting instructions alone does not establish
which resource dominates or a practical optimisation floor.

Likewise, the slower varied-board evaluation is consistent with poorer
lookup locality, but does not isolate cache misses, prove that reordering
cannot help, or demonstrate that future evaluation gains are exhausted.
The two rejected scratch rewrites do not establish those broader claims.

Append a correction distinguishing observations from hypotheses. A
supported conclusion is that no useful improvement was found in these two
small probes, and that pursuing fewer nodes is the chosen next direction.
If retaining the stronger mechanism/floor claims, supply supporting
measurements and the assumptions behind the cycle calculation. Further
experiments are **not required** if the conclusions are qualified instead.
Label the conditions repeated-subset versus varied-working-set, rather
than measured hot versus cold caches; label noisy pilot ranges as observed
ranges, not a demonstrated ±3 ns uncertainty bound.
