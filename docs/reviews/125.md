the strongest available proof that the copy is the 1.0.0 evaluator. The
agent description tests pin the evaluator name and threshold.

Required:

   original ran 100 playouts; this runs one game of at most 100 plies.

2. **Tie the copy to the original while both exist.** Add a test that

### Search rate (run by the reviewer)

The machine was quiet (load 1.6 to 1.8), so I ran the pre-registered
command unchanged at `838c8e2`. The log is
`docs/tuning/pattern_search_rate.log`, staged for you to commit with the
notes.

```text
classic positions 20 median_nps 3141479 median_depth 11
pattern positions 20 median_nps 2709403 median_depth 10
```

Over the 12 positions per evaluator that used the full three seconds (the
rest were solved early): classic 2.13 M nodes/s, mean depth 11.3; pattern
1.86 M nodes/s, mean depth 10.7. The pattern search runs at about **87%**
of the classic rate. The per-call cost is four times higher, but the
evaluator is a small part of a node. This is well above the half-rate
guideline: **no indexing work.**

### Rulings

1. **No re-fit before the gate.** 11% of sampled lookups are entries seen
   fewer than 20 times, and raw sums beyond the bound are 1 in 199. With
   the pattern agent scoring 83% at equal depth and 84% at equal nodes,
   there is no evidence the rare entries hurt, and changing the weights
   now would be tuning without a measured problem. The gate runs on the
   committed asset `676987…ea8cb`.
2. **Reading for the notes:** the tables cost about two thirds of a ply at
   3 s and are worth some 220 to 350 Elo at equal depth on the tune set.
   The gate measures the net.
